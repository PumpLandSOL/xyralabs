On chain,a paymentis a gap.

A shielded transaction proves that a payment happened without saying who sent it, who received it, or how much moved. Here's what a wallet does to make one, and what the chain keeps.

  1. 01 · Share an address

    The recipient hands over a post-quantum address

    The address carries an ML-KEM-768 public key. At about 1,900 characters it's long, so wallets share it as a QR code or a payment request.

  2. 02 · Seal the note

    The sender's wallet makes a fresh secret

    It encapsulates a shared secret for that key and uses it to encrypt the note, which holds the amount, a memo, and what the recipient needs to spend it later.

  3. 03 · Prove the spend

    The wallet builds a STARK

    The proof shows it owns an unspent note in the tree, that inputs equal outputs plus the fee, and that the proof belongs to this exact transaction.

  4. 04 · Verify and mine

    Nodes check the proof, miners fold it

    Every node confirms the nullifier has never been seen before. Miners fold all the proofs in a block into a single block proof.

  5. 05 · Receive

    One key opens one note

    The recipient's wallet tries its key on each new note. One opens. Nobody else can tell that anything arrived.

Sizeswedesignaround

Lasting privacyhas a size.

Post-quantum parts are bigger than the elliptic-curve parts they replace. These are the numbers, and how we keep them manageable.

  • Per transaction · estimate

    50–200 KB

    STARK spend proof, before it's folded into the block proof.

  • Per block

    1 proof

    Miners fold every transaction proof into one, so nodes verify a single proof per block.

  • Per address

    ~1,900

    Characters in an address. The ML-KEM-768 key alone is 1,184 bytes, so wallets use QR codes.

  • Trusted setups

    0

    STARKs are transparent. There's no ceremony and no secret that someone has to destroy.

Thingspeoplekeepasking

Questions.Answers.

Is anything really quantum-proof?
Nobody can promise forever. XYRA uses NIST's post-quantum standards and hash-based proofs, the most studied options available, and every part is designed to be upgraded if the research moves.
Why not ring signatures like Monero?
Post-quantum ring signatures exist, but they're large and hide each spend among only a small group. A shielded pool hides every spend among every note on the chain.
Can I prove a payment when I need to?
Yes. A viewing key shows chosen transactions to an auditor or an exchange without giving anyone the ability to spend.
When does mainnet launch?
After the specification is public and outside audits are complete. Follow along for testnet dates.
